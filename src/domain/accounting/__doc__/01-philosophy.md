# Philosophy

> This document is scoped to the following milestone:\
> https://github.com/oosahon/PurpleLedger-core/milestone/1 (v0.1.0 — Individual MVP)

## Reporting-First Approach

Our philosophy is to follow a reporting-first approach to accounting. This means that we will first determine the reporting requirements for each accounting domain and then create a set of ledger accounts that will allow us to generate the required reports. We will also create a set of behaviors that will be built into these ledger accounts. For non-power users, these accounts and behaviors will be bootstrapped while power users will have the ability to create accounts and associate behaviors available to the account class.

## The Analogy: Bookkeeper vs. Accountant

To understand the architecture of PurpleLedger, it helps to look at how real-world finance departments operate. The system is split into two distinct conceptual roles:

### 1. The Bookkeeper (Ledger Domain)

The bookkeeper sits at a desk recording raw facts. When a transaction happens, they record the debits and credits in the General Ledger (GL).

- **Standard-Agnostic**: They do not care about IFRS, GAAP, tax laws, or depreciation schedules.
- **Goal**: Ensure the math works ($Assets = Liabilities + Equity$) and that the bank accounts reconcile.
- **Implementation**: This is the `LedgerAccount` and `JournalEntry` structure in `src/domain/ledger`. It is pure, factual, and strictly standard-agnostic.

### 2. The Accountant (Accounting/Reporting Domain)

The accountant comes in at the end of the reporting period. They take the raw trial balance generated by the bookkeeper, apply the relevant regulatory frameworks, calculate standard-specific adjustments, and generate the final Financial Statements.

- **Context-Aware**: They care deeply about whether they are reporting in GAAP or IFRS, and which Fiscal Year it is.
- **Implementation**: This is the `ReportingContext` and `ReportingHeader` structure in `src/domain/accounting`.
